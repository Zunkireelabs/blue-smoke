The mark

Two shapes only: an outer flame and an inner flame knocked out of it. The reference layers a third tongue on the right — dropped deliberately, because it is the first thing to disappear at icon size and it costs the silhouette its clarity.

Gradient — matches the reference

Vertical fall from a cyan top to brand blue. This is what gives the reference its heat — a flat flame reads as a leaf.

REQUIRES A NEW TOKEN

Flat — token-pure today

Solid #1657D0. Ships without touching the approved palette, and is the safer option at very small sizes where a gradient compresses to mud.

NO PALETTE CHANGE

The gradient needs an eighth colour

#22C1F2 · proposed

#0E3E9A · dark
the fall

This is your call, and tokens.ts says so out loud.

Its own header reads: "do not add a colour here that is not one of these seven without asking." A cyan-to-blue gradient needs a light top, and none of the seven approved values is light enough — #E8F0FE and #DCE9FB are near-white tints, not a cyan. So the gradient version means adding #22C1F2 as an eighth brand colour.

It would be decorative-only — used in the mark and nowhere near text — so it never enters the AA contrast guard as a text pair. But it is still a brand decision, and OQ-7 was resolved on the existing seven.

Motion

A flame invites flicker, and flicker is the trap — jitter on a logo reads as a rendering fault, not as fire. Both options below move the core and leave the silhouette alone, so the mark is never distorted.

1

Core

The inner flame breathes from its base — 4.6s, scaling 0.90 → 1 vertically. The outer silhouette is completely static.

Reads as a flame burning steadily rather than guttering. The silhouette holding perfectly still is what makes it feel controlled rather than cheap.

RECOMMENDED

2

Lean

Core breathing, plus the whole flame leaning ±1.9° hinged at its base on a 6.2s cycle.

More alive, and the two cycles drift out of phase so it never visibly repeats. Slightly more risk of reading as "wobbling" on a small screen.

3

None

Static. What everyone with reduced motion enabled sees regardless.

Worth looking at honestly: the splash is on screen for under 400ms, and a flame this solid may simply not need help. If you can't tell the difference in the phone frames above, that is the answer.

What changes in the build

PR 1 is already on feature/P0-7.0-brand-mark-and-splash and green. Almost all of it survives — the mark was built as a swappable component.

PieceFateWhy
BootSplashScreen.tsx untouched Renders whatever BrandMark is.
LaunchScreen.storyboard untouched Points at an imageset; only the PNGs inside are regenerated.
navigation.tsx, tests, guards untouched Nothing asserts on path data.
BrandMark.tsx geometry + layers Two paths instead of hexagon-plus-bars; the bars become one inner flame.
generate-brand-assets.py rewrite the draw call Pillow has no bezier primitive — the flame has to be flattened to a polygon, and the gradient drawn per-scanline. Real work, roughly an hour.
tokens.ts +1 token, if gradient Only if you approve #22C1F2. Flat needs nothing.

And the structural rule from the last brief has to be corrected.

PR 1 was told bars must be Animated.Views, never SVG rects, because useNativeDriver doesn't work on react-native-svg props. That was only true because bars are rectangles — a flame cannot be a View.

The rule that actually generalises: each animated layer is its own <Svg> inside its own Animated.View, with the transform on the View. Here that means two layers — a static outer flame, and an inner flame that breathes. The native driver animates the View; the SVG never re-renders.
